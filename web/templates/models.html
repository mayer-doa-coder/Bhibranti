Why "hard" and not the overall score

About 60% of questions can be answered by checking whether the answer text appears in the passage. A model that learns only that trick scores well overall and understands nothing. The hard questions are the ones where that trick fails, so that column is the only honest one.

The clearest example

Similarity numbers + Logistic Regression has the best overall score here (0.730) and almost the worst hard score (0.490). Its weights show it leans on "does the answer appear in the passage". It learned the trick. Judged by the overall column it would look like the best model; it is closer to the least useful.

Why the tie at the top is real

The top two both score 0.610. We checked instead of guessing: on the 218 hard records they disagree on 28, and each is right on exactly 14 (McNemar, p = 1.000). So it is reported as a tie.

Why one model says "unreliable"

Bag of Words + linear SVM never finishes fitting. Raw word counts run up to 55 with no ceiling and the SVM cannot settle on that, even after 20,000 tries. TF-IDF scales everything to 1 and settles in seconds. We still report the score, marked unreliable, because why it fails shows exactly what TF-IDF's weighting is for.

Two small numbers you may notice

The ± spreads are worked out from the 3-decimal numbers in the log, so they can differ by 0.001 from the guide, which used full precision. And Bag of Words + Logistic Regression lands on exactly 0.591 — the same as the fuzzy rule — so its bar is grey. Matching a rule is not beating it.
